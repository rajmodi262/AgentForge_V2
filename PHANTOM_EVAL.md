## 3. Audit of verified evidence (precision)

The verifier proves a quote *exists* and *fits its stage*; it cannot prove the stage label is the best one or that the quote is about *this* project. So random verified evidence items were reviewed by hand: is the stage right, and is the quote about this project?

| | Round 1 (before fixes) | Round 2 (fresh sample, after fixes) |
|---|---|---|
| Fully correct | 25/40 (62.5%) | **31/40 (77.5%)** |
| Stage too low (conservative) | 7 | 4 |
| Stage too high (dangerous) | 2 | 2 |
| Not real evidence / wrong project | 6 | 3 |

Round 1 drove four rule fixes (network "interconnected" ≠ power; future tense never proves LIVE; upgrade an under-labelled event when its own quote states a higher stage; "TM Nxera" = Telekom Malaysia). Round 2 is a **fresh sample** that excluded every round-1 item, so its number is not inflated by tuning. Its findings ("seeks a loan", "will acquire land", "secured 264 acres", "solar deal with TNB") were then fixed and turned into regression tests. Most remaining errors are *conservative* — the system under-states a project's stage more often than it over-states it, the safe direction for a sales tool.

Labels are a first pass by an AI assistant (Claude) and should be confirmed by the team: `PHANTOM_AUDIT_SAMPLE_round1.csv`, `PHANTOM_AUDIT_SAMPLE_round2.csv`. The weakness still visible: a real quote about a *different* project in the same article (1–2 per 40).

## 6. Calibration backtest: does the score mean what it says?

History is replayed at **4 cutoffs** (2022-06-30, 2023-06-30, 2024-06-30, 2025-06-30). At each one, every project known by then is scored using **only the evidence published up to that date**, and the prediction is compared with what verified evidence shows afterwards (regular hunt + Firecrawl outcome scout). *Progressed* = verified BUILDING or LIVE evidence; otherwise not progressed.

**233 project-cutoff cases from 104 distinct projects.** A project scored at several cutoffs is not several independent projects, so every confidence interval is a bootstrap that resamples whole projects.

### 6.1 What the backtest found: the stated priors were too optimistic

Fitting uses the cutoffs old enough for a project to have broken ground (2022-06-30, 2023-06-30, 2024-06-30; at least 2 years of follow-up): 129 cases from 58 projects.

| Layer when scored | Stated prior | Cases | Progressed | Observed | Fitted prior |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ANNOUNCED | 25% | 95 | 17 | 18% | **19.1%** |
| SITE | 40% | 5 | 2 | 40% | **40.0%** |
| APPROVED | 50% | 0 | 0 | — | **50.0%** |
| POWER | 75% | 5 | 0 | 0% | **60.0%** |
| BUILDING | 88% | 10 | 10 | 100% | **92.0%** |
| LIVE | 98% | 14 | 14 | 100% | **98.8%** |

*Fitted* = (progressed + 20 × stated prior) / (cases + 20), then forced to rise with the ladder. The 20 pseudo-cases mean a layer needs about 20 real cases before the data outweighs the assumption — so the well-populated ANNOUNCED layer moves a lot and the thin layers barely move. It also offsets a known bias: *no verified progress* under-counts real progress.

**The scorer now runs on the fitted priors** (`app/phantom/calibration.json`).

### 6.2 The fair test: projects NOT yet under construction when scored

105 cases from 44 projects; 18% went on to verified construction or operation. The fitted score is **cross-validated**: each project is scored with priors fitted on the *other* projects only (5 folds).

| Predictor | Brier (lower is better) | AUC (ranking; 0.5 = coin flip) |
|---|---|---|
| **Reality score, fitted priors (out of sample)** | **0.1556** (95% CI 0.082–0.237) | **0.588** (95% CI 0.378–0.784) |
| **Reality score, stated priors (as first built)** | **0.1565** (95% CI 0.091–0.229) | **0.646** (95% CI 0.468–0.819) |
| Evidence layer alone (stated prior, no adjustments) | 0.1689 | 0.367 |
| Flat 35% for every project (Bernstein's pipeline figure) | 0.1768 | 0.500 |
| Always the base rate (18%) — only knowable with hindsight | 0.1482 | 0.500 |

Fitted, out of sample:

| Predicted score | Cases | Mean predicted | Observed progressed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-20% | 86 | 13% | 14% |
| 20-40% | 12 | 24% | 17% |
| 40-60% | 5 | 52% | 40% |
| 60-80% | 2 | 61% | 0% |

Stated priors:

| Predicted score | Cases | Mean predicted | Observed progressed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-20% | 79 | 17% | 13% |
| 20-40% | 19 | 26% | 21% |
| 40-60% | 3 | 51% | 67% |
| 60-80% | 4 | 69% | 0% |

**What this does and does not show.**

* **As a filter it works.** Of 98 cases the fitted score called phantom risk (below 45%), **81 (83%) showed no verified progress** afterwards, and its low scores are calibrated (see the first row of the table).
* **It beats a flat 35% and the evidence layer alone**, but it does **not** beat always guessing the base rate — a number nobody has in advance. Before construction starts, the evidence in the news separates projects only weakly: the AUC interval includes 0.5.
* **It cannot yet pick winners early.** With the stated priors 4 pre-construction case(s) scored 60% or more and 0 progressed; with fitted priors 2 do. The honest reading is *validated for ruling projects out, not for ruling them in*.
* *Not progressed* means no verified evidence was found, which is weaker than proof of failure, so observed rates are a lower bound.

### 6.5 What would make this conclusive

* **More outcomes.** The next scout should chase 2019–2023 announcements specifically, to reach a few hundred independent projects.
* **Stronger pre-construction evidence.** News rarely separates serious projects from talk before ground is broken; filings do (supply agreements, environmental clearances, land registrations). That is where the next data source should come from.
* **Per-developer track records** (see `analytics/ANALYSIS.md`) as a prior, once enough developers have five or more judged projects.

## 7. Power forensics: coverage, not yet precision

`app/phantom/power.py` reads each project's sentences for grid position, supplier, connection, on-site generation, cooling, and who builds and funds it. It is rules only (no LLM), every fact keeps its quote, and facts pass the same subject check as the evidence — plus two more: a company name only counts when the sentence names the project's developer, and a sentence the miner attached to two projects in one article is kept only where it points (facility code, city or capacity).

**191 facts on 84 of 371 projects (23%).** By field: cooling 57, renewable 41, substation 21, utility 14, grid_mva 11, grid_kv 10, financier 10, grid 8, onsite 6, water 6, contractor 3, pue 2, rack_kw 2.

| Grid position | Projects | Announced MW |
|---|---|---|
| Energized (operating) | 68 | 6,381 |
| Grid agreement signed | 7 | 2,716 |
| Power deal, grid not confirmed | 23 | 7,114 |
| Under construction, power agreement not found | 26 | 4,057 |
| No public evidence of power | 247 | 50,824 |

Secured (operating, or a verified supply agreement): **9,097 MW of 71,092 MW (13%)**. Among projects not yet operating: 2,716 MW (4%) across 7 projects.

**How to read the gap.** *No public evidence* means the sources say nothing about power — it is not proof that no agreement exists; supply agreements are often unannounced. So the secured share is a floor, and the low coverage is itself the result: news alone cannot answer the power question for most of the pipeline.

**Precision is not claimed.** PHANTOM_POWER_AUDIT_SAMPLE.csv holds a random sample of facts with empty label columns (is the fact stated? is it about this project?) for two people to grade independently. Unit tests pin the rules on the failure cases found while building them ("carrier-neutral" is not the Carrier company, a cable landing station is not a substation, "avoiding evaporative cooling" is not evaporative cooling).

**Developer track records** (`app/phantom/track.py`, `GET /phantom/developers`): 10 developers have 3+ projects old enough to judge (first reported 24+ months ago). The table is in `analytics/ANALYSIS.md`.

## 8. What is not measured yet (and how it would be)

- **Whether the score can pick winners early.** §6 validates it as a filter and shows it cannot yet separate pre-construction projects better than the base rate. That needs a few hundred independent outcomes and stronger pre-construction evidence (filings).
- **Precision of the power facts.** §7 reports coverage; `PHANTOM_POWER_AUDIT_SAMPLE.csv` is a random sample with empty label columns for people to grade. No precision figure is claimed until they have.
- **Human labels for §3.** The evidence audit is still a first pass by an AI assistant and should be relabelled by two people independently.
- **Recall on small projects.** The reference list in §5 comes from published lists, which favour large operators; an analyst's full list for one region would measure the long tail.
